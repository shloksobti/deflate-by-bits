The Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) raises the significance bar with the number of strategies tried, and recent work logs every backtest so DSR can be applied to the true search intensity. We show that counting backtests is the wrong currency when the researcher is adaptive, as in everyday quantitative practice, LLM research agents and RL alpha miners. We prove that a "combine what worked" procedure over k pure-noise strategies drives the validation t-statistic to sqrt(2k/pi), while the DSR bar grows only as sqrt(2 ln k), so query-counted DSR certifies noise with probability tending to one. Conversely, if the holdout can reveal only a transcript from a set T, certifying at the alpha/|T| quantile is valid for any adaptive analyst; DSR is the non-adaptive special case, and overfitting scales with the square root of the bits revealed. This motivates a sealed holdout whose only operation is a budgeted PASS/FAIL verdict. On synthetic markets and 423 real industry strategies (1970-2026) under a bootstrap null, ordinary adaptive workflows are DSR-certified on 84-100% of noise replicates, while sealed verdicts hold false certification at 1-3%. Frontier coding agents (Claude Code) usually decline to tune on the holdout, but when instructed to maximise validation Sharpe, Claude Opus executes our attack autonomously in about a minute, reaching validation t up to 15.7 on noise (test t near 0); ledger DSR certifies these searches under optional stopping. A GRPO alpha miner with a pool reward learns the same ensemble and is DSR-certified. In multi-round campaigns that reuse one holdout, adaptive full-precision reuse makes a false discovery in 100% of null campaigns, while the sealed verdict stays at 1-3% with power 0.65-0.79.
