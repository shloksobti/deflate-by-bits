Table~\ref{tab:e5} and Figure~\ref{fig:grpo} report three seeds (independent null replicates and RL seeds) for each validation-rewarded null arm, and one seed for the remaining arms.

\paragraph{Validation reward: overfitting by selection, which DSR handles.} In all three null seeds the policy quickly converges on a small family of portfolios with validation $t$ of 2.3--3.9 and test $t$ between $-0.61$ and $0.40$. This is overfitting by \emph{selecting} a lucky portfolio, the non-adaptive case for which DSR was designed, and ledger DSR correctly declines in every seed (0.34--0.68). On actual data the best portfolio, first generated at step 187, has validation $t=5.00$ (a 2000--2012 annualised Sharpe of 1.39) and then \emph{loses money} over 2013--2026 (test $t=-0.26$). DSR declines to certify it, but by a margin of only $0.002$ (0.948). A second actual-data seed was stopped early to free GPU memory and is excluded.

\paragraph{Pool reward: RL learns the ensemble attack, which defeats DSR.} The AlphaGen-style pool reward pays for \emph{combination}, and in one of three null seeds that is what the policy learns. In seeds 1 and 2 the policy loses entropy within about 20 steps and the pool stalls (validation $t$ of 4.12 and 2.86; in seed 1 six of seven signals are variants of a single industry). In seed 3 the policy keeps exploring (9{,}201 distinct portfolios) and the pool grows steadily from 2 to 177 signals spanning 37 of the 47 industries (one to eight signals each). Its validation $t$ rises from 0.4 to 12.0. For comparison, Theorem~\ref{thm:attack} gives $\sqrt{2\cdot177/\pi}\approx10.6$ for an equal-weight sign-selected ensemble of that size; the pool's continuous weights make this a heuristic benchmark rather than a prediction. Its test $t$ stays near zero ($-0.56$). Ledger DSR over all distinct portfolios and pool states certifies it with $\mathrm{DSR}=1.000$. An RL alpha miner has thus rediscovered the attack of Theorem~\ref{thm:attack} from the reward signal alone and obtained a DSR certificate for pure noise.

\paragraph{Training reward with a sealed holdout.} With training reward the miner overfits the \emph{training} period instead. On actual data it converges to a basket of 5- and 21-day industry momentum signals with a 1970--1999 annualised Sharpe of 4.3, consistent with the strong short-horizon autocorrelation of pre-2000 daily industry returns. Its validation and test Sharpe are about zero, and the sealed verdict over the 40 best-by-training portfolios certifies nothing in either train-reward run.

Whether an RL miner defeats ledger DSR therefore depends on whether its optimiser happens to keep exploring, which is a property of the seed and the entropy schedule, not of the evidence. The sealed reward makes validity independent of such details.
